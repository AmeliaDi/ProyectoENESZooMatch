**Notes:** Widely validated instruments (DAS, RAS, CSI, PRQC) have robust psychometric support (factor analyses, high alphas). The TLS-45 and its short form TLS-15 have demonstrated cross-cultural invariance. The Passionate Love Scale and Rubin’s Love Scale are older, widely used measures of romantic love; they have shown high reliability and validity in many studies. Some instruments (e.g. PRQC) are available freely; others (e.g. DAS) are copyrighted and require formal use permissions.

# HEXACO Traits and Romantic Relationships

Research specifically linking the HEXACO dimensions to romantic satisfaction is still emerging, but findings align with broader personality theories.  In general, **Honesty–Humility** and **Agreeableness** tend to facilitate trust and cooperation in couples, which likely enhances relationship quality.  For example, one large study of singles found that people prefer ideal partners who are similar to themselves in HEXACO traits and particularly higher in Honesty–Humility and Openness.  Honesty–Humility (fairness, sincerity) has been theorized to build trust and loyalty in partnerships, whereas Agreeableness (forgiveness, flexibility) helps resolve conflicts. 

Conversely, high **Emotionality** (akin to Big Five Neuroticism, reflecting anxiety and sensitivity) may predict lower satisfaction, since emotional volatility can strain relationships.  **Extraversion** and **Conscientiousness** might modestly contribute (e.g. sociability and reliability could support positive interactions), but evidence is scant.  Overall, the HEXACO profile most conducive to healthy relationships appears to involve high H–H and A (for selfless, cooperative behavior) and low E (for emotional stability).

Empirical work specifically using HEXACO scales in couples is limited.  Spanish researchers have used the HEXACO-60 in community samples (e.g. Roncero et al., 2014), indicating validated Spanish versions exist, though these studies focused on psychopathology rather than relationships.  To date, strong links have been suggested but not firmly established through large studies. More targeted research is needed. One open finding is that **Honesty–Humility** often correlates with relationship-relevant outcomes: for instance, it relates negatively to relational aggression and positively to mutual support. 

**In summary:** HEXACO-60 and -100 have been used in diverse contexts, and H–H and A consistently emerge as important for social harmony.  Available evidence (e.g. singles’ preferences) implies partners high in H–H (sincere, fair) and low in exploitative tendencies tend to have higher relationship satisfaction, though direct citations in couples studies remain few.  Spanish adaptations of the HEXACO (via HEXACO-60) are available and show expected factor structure, supporting their use in relationship research.  However, detailed psychometric studies linking HEXACO profiles to measures like the DAS or CSI are not yet prominent in the literature. Future studies should examine how each HEXACO trait predicts satisfaction, commitment, similarity, and other relational variables.
